# Centrifugal Pump Maintenance Manual

## 4.1 Routine inspection

Record suction pressure, discharge pressure, flow, motor current, rotational speed, vibration RMS, and bearing temperature under stable process conditions. Compare trends against the machine baseline rather than relying on one isolated reading.

## 6.2 Cavitation indicators

Cavitation may produce fluctuating vibration, a crackling sound, unstable discharge pressure, falling suction pressure, and reduced flow. Check inlet isolation valves, strainers, liquid level, and suction restrictions. Do not continue operation when inspection indicates a risk of equipment damage.

## 7.3 Bearing vibration and temperature

Concurrent increases in drive-end vibration and bearing temperature can indicate bearing degradation, lubrication problems, looseness, or shaft misalignment. A rising trend is more significant than a single elevated sample.

Before physical inspection, follow the approved lockout/tagout procedure. Inspect lubricant condition, bearing housing, coupling, fasteners, and shaft alignment. Bearing replacement is justified only after the evidence and inspection findings are reviewed by qualified personnel.

## 7.4 Shaft misalignment

Misalignment commonly produces elevated vibration after installation or coupling work and may increase bearing temperature and motor current. Verify soft foot, baseplate condition, pipe strain, and cold alignment before correcting the coupling.

## 8.1 Motor overload

High motor current combined with reduced rotational speed or abnormal temperature can indicate excessive process load, mechanical binding, incorrect operating point, or electrical problems. Check the approved operating envelope and motor protection before further operation.
